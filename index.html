Britain’s shopping basket is shrinking – but we are eating out more than ever

After the cost of living crisis, households are buying less of almost everything at the supermarket. Yet the money is still flowing – into restaurants, cafés and takeaways.

A typical person in the UK spent £50.03 a week on food and drink in the financial year ending March 2025 (FYE 2025), according to Defra’s Family Food survey. Once inflation is stripped out, that is 2.7% more than a year earlier. But where the money goes has changed.

02 | SPENDING

Figure 1 shows the split. Adjusted for inflation, weekly spending on food and drink eaten out rose from £10.61 to £12.69 per person between FYE 2022 and FYE 2025 – a rise of almost 20%. Spending on food for the home slipped from £38.13 to £37.34. Eating out now makes up a quarter of the total, still short of the 32% recorded before the pandemic.

04 | CONTEXT

Compared with FYE 2022, alcoholic drinks (about a fifth fewer), soft drinks (more than 13% fewer) and milk (about 11% fewer) saw the sharpest falls. Meat was one of the very few groups to rise, by around 3%; Defra notes that all but two of the main food groups it tracks are down over the period.

Prices are part of the explanation: in March 2025, food and non-alcoholic drink prices rose 4% a year, faster than the 3.4% rate for all items. The burden is uneven. The poorest fifth of households spent 15.2% of their budget on food at home, against 10.9% for the average household.

A note of caution: Defra warns that diary surveys tend to under-report purchases, especially eating out and top-up shopping, and that pandemic-era changes to data collection may affect some categories.
